Press release

House of Deceit brings social deduction to the British weekend away

WHITSTABLE, KENT — A new two-night immersive getaway is inviting groups to leave London behind, move into a private house by the sea and discover how well they really know their friends.

House of Deceit is a hosted social game played throughout an entire stay. Guests are secretly assigned roles before a cycle of murders, trials, executions, challenges, alliances and betrayals begins. The game runs around the group rather than on a stage: private conversations matter, every accusation has consequences and nobody can be certain who is telling the truth.

The experience has been created for the millions of viewers who enjoy television programmes such as The Traitors, but want to take part themselves. House of Deceit is an independent production and is not affiliated with or endorsed by the BBC or The Traitors.

Unlike a conventional evening event, guests stay inside the game for two nights. The first House of Deceit is a large private property in Whitstable, Kent, around 15 minutes' walk from the station and approximately one hour by train from London. Welcome pizzas are included on the first evening, while the house's large kitchen allows groups to cater for themselves for the rest of the stay.

“People do not need another night where everyone looks at their phones. We wanted to create the kind of weekend that takes over the group chat for months afterwards — because your closest friend framed you for murder.” James Gordon, founder of House of Deceit

House of Deceit is aimed at birthdays, reunions, celebrations and groups searching for an unusual UK weekend away. Standard bookings are for 10 to 15 guests. Selected midweek stays start at £126 per person, with Friday and bank-holiday packages priced at £166 per person.

Dates are available during August and September 2026. Further dates and locations are planned as the concept grows.
